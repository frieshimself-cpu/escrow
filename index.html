How it works

Three steps between an idea and a claimable token

Deployers kick things off. Escrow keeps everyone honest. Creators collect what's theirs — whenever they show up.

01

Anyone deploys

Pick a creator's X handle, name the token, and launch. You don't need their permission — and you never touch their share.

02

Escrow locks the supply

A fixed slice of total supply is sealed in a program-owned vault at mint. It's visible to everyone and spendable by no one.

03

The creator claims

The named creator signs in with X, proves the handle is theirs, and the vault releases directly to their wallet. No middlemen.

Creator protection

The creator's cut is untouchable — even by the deployer

Every SupplyFun launch reserves a share of supply that no key on earth can move except the verified creator's. Deployers can hype, trade, and build — they just can't take what isn't theirs.

  • Escrow vault created atomically with the mint
  • Deployer wallets are program-restricted from the reserved share
  • Unclaimed supply stays sealed indefinitely — no expiry rug
Verified handoff

Proof of handle, not proof of promise

Claiming runs through an X OAuth sign-in bound to the handle named at deploy. If the account doesn't match, the vault doesn't open. Screenshots and impersonators get nothing.

  • OAuth-verified match between handle and claimant
  • Claim transaction settles straight to the creator's wallet
  • Every claim is publicly inspectable on-chain
Radical transparency

Everything happens where everyone can see it

Escrow balances, claim events, milestone unlocks, liquidity routing — all of it lives on-chain. If you can read an explorer, you can audit the whole lifecycle of every launch.

  • Program-owned vaults with public balances
  • Milestone rules committed at deploy, immutable after
  • No admin keys over creator allocations
